Overview

The eco-evolutionary framework.

Cancer is a living, evolving community inside a human host. Understanding it — predicting it, steering it, treating it — requires reading it across three coupled scales at once.

The premise

An ecological community, not an invader.

For most of modern oncology, cancer has been described as an invasion — cells breaching boundaries, colonizing tissues, spreading through the body. The language is so familiar it has become invisible. But measured against the biology, the invasion model is wrong by one to two orders of magnitude. Real tumors expand at a small fraction of their cells' intrinsic potential. They maintain sharp boundaries. They engineer their surroundings. They communicate risk across their edges. They depend on stable internal resources and can crash episodically under perturbation. These are not the properties of an invader. They are the properties of a community.

The Cancer Ecology Center reads cancer through the lens of ecology and evolution — the same lens used to study any community of organisms competing, cooperating, and adapting within a defined environment. The patient is the environment. The tumor is a community of cells, with their own interactions, fitness landscapes, and dynamics. The carcinogens, drugs, and immune cells that pass through it are forces in that ecology.

This reframing is not metaphorical. It carries a specific mathematical and clinical commitment. It says cancer can be modeled with the formal tools that ecology and evolutionary biology have spent a century developing — generalized Lotka-Volterra systems, evolutionary game theory, fitness-generating functions, Bayesian state-space models. And it says the right clinical goal is not to eradicate the community but to steer it: at the level of exposure, at the level of tumor ecology, and at the level of individualized forecast.

One sentence holds the program together: steering, not eradication, at every scale — reroute the exposure flux, suppress the resistant lineage with the sensitive one, assimilate sparse clinical data into a living model of the patient. The rest of the site is what that means in practice. The three coupled scales below are how we organize the work.

The framework

Three coupled scales of one biology.

Cancer is read at three depths — what determines who gets it, how it actually behaves once it starts, and how it can be steered for an individual. Each scale has its own falsifiable claims and its own open questions. The work of the Center is the integration across them.

Layer 01 · Origin

Exposure

What determines who gets cancer in the first place?

Cancer emerges from combinatorial flux through tissue-specific bioactivation networks, modulated by inherited variants, clonal mosaicism, and lifetime exposure history. Not one chemical. Not one gene.

Layer 02 · Behavior

Ecosystem

Once it starts, how does it actually behave?

The autonomous cancer cell is a new species. The tumor is a town — bounded, habitat-engineering, resource-depleting, edge-managed. Invasion analogies are off by one to two orders of magnitude.

Layer 03 · Forecast

Assimilation

How do we predict and steer it for an individual?

Tumors are ecosystems with latent interaction matrices. The correct clinical instrument is not a static biomarker panel but a data-assimilating ecological digital twin, continuously re-estimating from sparse clinical signal.
