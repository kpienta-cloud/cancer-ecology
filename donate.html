Support the Center

Your help enables discovery.

Philanthropy keeps science moving between grants. Every gift to the Cancer Ecology Center funds the experiments, the trainees, and the early-stage work that federal funding does not yet support.

A note from the co-directors

Thank you for considering us.

Every important discovery in this laboratory began as an idea no grant agency would yet fund. Private philanthropy is what kept those ideas alive long enough to mature into the work you read about today — from polyploid giant cells to the cancer digital twin to the isotope-based early-detection program.

When you give, you are not buying a piece of equipment or paying a salary. You are giving the laboratory permission to take a risk — to test something difficult, to follow a hunch, to train a young scientist on a question that matters. That is the work we cannot do without you.

We are grateful for every gift, however large or small. We will tell you what your gift did. And we will keep showing up for the men and families we serve, every day.

With deep thanks,
Ken & Sarah

Where your gift goes

Three pillars, one program.

Gifts support the Center as a whole, but it is useful to know what that means in practice. Below is what each pillar of the framework needs most right now.

§ Risk

Exposure & susceptibility.

Carcinogen mapping, exposure-genotype integration, and the cohort science that links environment to disease. Your gift here funds the BGES cohort and the ExposoGraph / GeoToxGraph instruments — the science that maps who is at risk and why.

§ Detection

Catching cancer earlier.

Hair-isotope screening, CTCs, ctDNA, and the disseminated-tumor-cell biology that makes recurrence detectable before it is incurable. Your gift here funds the IsoMCED early-detection program and the Amend Lab's dormancy work.

§ Treatment

Steering, not eradication.

The Cancer Ecology Digital Twin (CEDT), the Prairie-Dogs G-function engine, and adaptive-therapy trials. Your gift here funds the computational backbone of precision care — the science of how we treat lethal cancer.

In the long run

A gift that outlasts a grant.

The Donald S. Coffey Professorship — held by Dr. Pienta — was endowed by the gifts of patients and friends who wanted to ensure that the science Don Coffey began would continue for generations. That is the model. A named gift, a fund, a chair: these are the things that survive the grants, the politics, and the years.

If a legacy gift interests you, see the Coffey legacy page and write to the Development Office to discuss.
