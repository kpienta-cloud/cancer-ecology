The program

An eco-evolutionary research program at Johns Hopkins.

The Cancer Ecology Center is the institutional home for the framework. The 16-institution Cancer Ecology Consortium is the collaborative network that extends it. Together they answer the program's three questions across three coupled scales.

§ The Center

What it is.

The Cancer Ecology Center is housed at the Brady Urological Institute at Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine. It exists to develop, test, and translate the eco-evolutionary framework for cancer: a single program that reads cancer across risk, detection, and treatment — and steers it at every scale.

The Center's mandate is concrete. Develop the instruments (ExposoGraph, GeoToxGraph, Prairie-Dogs, CEDT, IsoMCED). Produce the falsifiable predictions. Run the cohort science (BGES). Translate the deployable diagnostics (IsoMCED). Train the next generation of computational oncologists who can hold all three pillars in their head at once.

The Center sits at Hopkins because that is where the clinical infrastructure, the cohort, and the historical roots of this work all are. It is not bound to Hopkins — its consortium partnerships are global by design.

§ The Consortium

16 institutions, one framework.

The Cancer Ecology Consortium is a 16-institution collaborative network organized around the framework. It exists because the questions are too big for any single laboratory, the cohorts are too small at any single site to test the falsifiable predictions, and the methods are too cross-disciplinary to live inside a single department.

The Consortium's role is shared infrastructure, shared cohorts, shared methods, and shared accountability — the falsification tests are pre-registered across the network, not on the Center's desk. The institutional roster will be published with the v6.5 CEDT manuscript.

Institutions and investigators interested in joining the Consortium are encouraged to write directly. The on-ramp is a structured collaboration agreement and a commitment to the pre-registered analytic protocols.

§ Training

The Brady SURE Program.

The Brady Summer Undergraduate Research Experience (SURE) brings undergraduates into the laboratory for a full summer of hands-on research under the mentorship of a faculty member and their team. Students are matched to ongoing projects in cancer ecology, dormancy biology, computational systems oncology, or clinical-translational science.

The program is competitive and limited in size. We host a small cohort each year because we believe close mentorship matters more than volume. SURE students present their work at the end of the summer and frequently continue as authors on the resulting publications.

Applications open each spring. Inquiries from prospective students should be sent to kpienta1@jhmi.edu with a brief statement of interest and a CV.

§ Our commitment

Diversity, equity, and inclusion.

The Cancer Ecology Center is committed to building a laboratory and a consortium that reflects the breadth of the populations our science serves. We affirm that cancer disparities are real, that the people most affected by them are under-represented in cancer research, and that closing both gaps is a scientific responsibility — not a side project.

Our hiring, training, and authorship practices are designed to be transparent and equitable. Our cohort science explicitly recruits and retains under-represented populations, and our published findings are stratified by ancestry and exposure context whenever sample size allows. We do not tolerate discrimination or harassment in any form.

  • Open recruitment for every position, with broad outreach to minority-serving institutions and training programs.
  • Mentorship that meets trainees where they are — not where the lab assumes they should be.
  • Cohort and consortium design that recruits, retains, and reports on under-represented populations.
  • Authorship and credit that reflect contribution, not seniority alone.
  • Zero tolerance for discrimination or harassment, with reporting routed through the Johns Hopkins Office of Institutional Equity.

§ Join us

Open positions and inquiries.

We are open to investigator, postdoctoral, graduate, and undergraduate collaborations across all three layers — and to consortium-level partnerships from institutions with cohorts or methods that test the framework. The fastest path is a short email describing what you do and the layer that excites you.

Postdoctoral fellows with backgrounds in cancer biology, dormancy, computational systems biology, evolutionary biology, exposomics, or stable-isotope geochemistry are particularly encouraged to write.

Graduate students at Hopkins can rotate through the lab via the Cellular and Molecular Medicine, Pathobiology, or Biomedical Engineering programs. Prospective students should write before applying.

Undergraduates should review the Brady SURE Program for the summer track.

Consortium institutions interested in joining the 16-institution network should write directly to the co-directors.
