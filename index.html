Cancer is an ecological community.

Reading it across exposure, ecosystem, and forecast — and steering it at every scale — is the program.

The framework

Three coupled scales of one biology.

Cancer is read at three depths — what determines who gets it, how it actually behaves once it starts, and how it can be steered for an individual. Each scale has its own falsifiable claims and its own open questions. The work of the Center is the integration across them.

Layer 01 · Origin

Exposure

What determines who gets cancer in the first place?

Cancer emerges from combinatorial flux through tissue-specific bioactivation networks, modulated by inherited variants, clonal mosaicism, and lifetime exposure history. Not one chemical. Not one gene.

Read the layer
Layer 02 · Behavior

Ecosystem

Once it starts, how does it actually behave?

The autonomous cancer cell is a new species. The tumor is a town — bounded, habitat-engineering, resource-depleting, edge-managed. Invasion analogies are off by one to two orders of magnitude.

Read the layer
Layer 03 · Forecast

Assimilation

How do we predict and steer it for an individual?

Tumors are ecosystems with latent interaction matrices. The correct clinical instrument is not a static biomarker panel but a data-assimilating ecological digital twin, continuously re-estimating from sparse clinical signal.

Steering, not eradication

Where the work is now

Open questions across the framework.

A research program is defined less by what it knows than by what it is actively trying to answer. These are the questions the Center is working on now.

Origin

How much of an individual's cancer risk is explained by combinatorial carcinogen flux through tissue-specific networks, versus inherited variants, versus clonal hematopoiesis?

Behavior

What makes a tumor's bounded ecology stable — and what perturbations cause it to crash, recover, or evade adaptive therapy?

Read the Behavior layer

Forecast

Can a Bayesian digital twin assimilate sparse, irregular clinical data well enough to forecast — and steer — an individual patient's tumor trajectory?

The integrating claim

Each layer publishes its own falsifiable predictions. The program publishes one shared one: steering, not eradication, holds at every scale — for exposure, for tumor ecology, and for clinical forecast.
