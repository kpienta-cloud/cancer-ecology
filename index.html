The pillars

Three pillars of an ecosystem program.

The clinical arc — Risk, Detection, Treatment — is the visible architecture of the Center. Underneath it sits the eco-evolutionary methodology that lets us hold all three at once: combinatorial exposure, the cancer cell as a new species, the tumor as a bounded ecosystem. Each pillar has its own falsifiable claims and its own open questions; the work of the Center is the integration across them.

Pillar 01 · Risk

Genes & Exposures

What determines who gets cancer in the first place?

Risk is combinatorial flux through tissue-specific networks, modulated by inherited variants, clonal mosaicism, and lifetime exposure. Not one chemical. Not one gene. The right object is the swamp the host tissue has become.

Pillar 02 · Detection

of the new cancer species

How do we catch it early enough to change its trajectory?

Once autonomous, the cancer cell is a new biological species. We detect it the way ecologists detect a new invader — through trace signals in the host environment: hair-keratin isotopes, ctDNA, CTCs, PSMA imaging, dormant disseminated cells.

Read the layer
Pillar 03 · Treatment

of lethal cancer

How do we treat metastatic disease when eradication has failed?

Lethal cancer is a bounded ecosystem with latent interaction dynamics. The right instrument is not maximal cytoreduction but ecological steering — adaptive therapy, evolution-aware dosing, and a continuously assimilating digital twin.

Where the work is now

Open questions across the framework.

A research program is defined less by what it knows than by what it is actively trying to answer. These are the questions the Center is working on now.

Risk

How much of an individual's lifetime cancer risk is explained by combinatorial carcinogen flux through tissue-specific networks, versus inherited variants, versus clonal hematopoiesis?

Read the Risk pillar

Detection

Can a single, non-invasive readout — a hair-isotope fingerprint, a liquid biopsy, an imaging signal — detect a nascent cancer species early enough to intercept it?

Read the Detection pillar

Treatment

Can we treat metastatic cancer as a steerable bounded ecosystem rather than an enemy to eradicate — and double or triple time-to-progression by doing so?

Read the Treatment pillar

The integrating claim

An eco-evolutionary framework lets us hold all three pillars at once. Predict who is at risk. Detect the new species before it establishes. Intercept and steer lethal disease rather than try to eradicate it.
